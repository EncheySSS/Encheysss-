Historical Highlights

Founding & Monastic Roots (1909)

The school was founded on October 12, 1909, by Chogyal Sidkeong Tulku to provide Buddhist novice monks with both traditional monastic study and modern subjects. Over the decades, it welcomed Tibetan refugee children in 1959 and opened its doors to all communities of Sikkim with formal CBSE affiliation in 1970.

School Life & Co-Curriculars

Enchey balances modern academic excellence with rich Himalayan culture. Students actively participate in traditional Thangka scroll painting, indigenous archery, folk dance, Bharat Scouts and Guides, eco-initiatives, and competitive sports.

Infrastructure & Campus

Situated on a peaceful ridge overlooking Gangtok and Mount Kanchenjunga, our campus combines traditional Sikkimese architecture with modern facilities, science and computer labs, an all-weather covered assembly ground, and student boarding hostels.

About Enchey Senior Secondary School

Our Legacy & Ethos

Enchey Senior Secondary School holds a landmark position in Sikkim's educational journey. Established in 1909, it originated as a unique experiment in monastic education initiated by the visionary ruler Chogyal Sidkeong Tulku. Over the decades, it transformed into a premier public co-educational boarding school under the Directorate of Education, Government of Sikkim.

Nestled on a serene hilltop ridge overlooking the city of Gangtok and the majestic Kanchenjunga peak, the institution provides an inspiring environment where academic rigor meets rich Himalayan cultural heritage.

Academic Environment & Pedagogy

The school is committed to nurturing holistic learning by combining standard national curricula with localized, context-rich knowledge systems. With state-funded modern infrastructure, qualified faculty, and dedicated residential facilities, Enchey Senior Secondary School serves as an educational beacon for students hailing from both urban Gangtok and remote mountain districts.

  • Academic Excellence: Delivering strong foundational knowledge in STEM, Commerce, and Humanities under the CBSE framework to empower students for national competitive standards.
  • Cultural Roots & Identity: Preserving the unique multi-ethnic heritage, languages, and values of the Eastern Himalayas.
  • Inclusive Education: Providing equal educational access, subsidized boarding facilities, and support for rural students across Sikkim's remote districts.
  • Mindfulness & Character Building: Fostering compassion, intellectual integrity, and community service inspired by the school's historical monastic foundations.

Historical Timeline & Leadership

1. Founding & Monastic Roots (1909)

  • Visionary Royal Founder: On October 12, 1909, Chogyal (King) Sidkeong Tulku laid the foundation for the school.
  • Purpose: Created specifically for young Buddhist monks to combine traditional monastic studies with modern subjects like basic arithmetic, grammar, poetry, medicine, and astrology.
  • Early Setup: Operating out of the Enchey Monastery compound with surrounding huts as dormitories, it initially enrolled 5 monks—including Kazi Lhendup Dorjee Khangsarpa, Sikkim's future first Chief Minister.

2. Expansion to Tibetan Refugees (1959)

  • Tibetan Influx: Following political developments in Tibet in 1959, the school broadened its mandate by enrolling Tibetan refugee children alongside monastic students.
  • New Campus (1966): To accommodate the growing student body, the Tibetan Government-in-Exile constructed a dedicated building in 1966, forming the basis of the modern campus site.

3. Integration & Formal Recognition (1969–1970s)

  • New Leadership & CBSE Affiliation: Under Principal Madhusudhan Singh in 1969, the curriculum was modernized, earning official recognition from the Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) in 1970.
  • Opening to All Communities (1970): Admissions opened to children of all backgrounds, ethnic groups, and communities across Sikkim, morphing into a multicultural co-educational boarding school. Its first 12th-grade CBSE batch graduated in 1973.

4. Shift to Modern Mainstream Schooling (1980s–Present)

  • Curriculum Expansion: Nepali was introduced into the curriculum during 1984–85 to align with state requirements.
  • Modern Era: By the early 1990s, monastic enrollments gradually wound down, shifting the school fully into mainstream public education.
  • Cultural Identity: The school honors its heritage through cultural education, traditional attire (such as the Bakhu), and regional language studies.

School Activities & Student Life

Cultural & Traditional Arts

  • Thangka Painting Workshops: Hands-on training under traditional artists teaching classical Buddhist scroll painting, symmetry, and natural pigment art.
  • Folk Music & Dance Groups: Student troupes training in traditional Sikkimese, Bhutia, Lepcha, and Nepali folk performance arts for state celebrations.
  • Festivals & Ritual Performances: School participation in Pang Lhabsol, Losar, Saga Dawa, and Bhanu Jayanti events.
  • Traditional Attire Days: Regular practice of wearing the traditional Sikkimese dress (Bakhu or Kho) to build pride in regional identity.

Sports & Outdoor Adventure

  • Traditional Archery: Practice sessions and competitions keeping traditional Himalayan wooden-bow archery alive among youth.
  • Inter-House Athletic Championships: Annual multi-day sports meets covering football, basketball, volleyball, table tennis, and track & field.
  • Trekking & Nature Trails: Guided mountain treks and outdoor survival excursions across local ridge tracks around Gangtok.
  • Martial Arts & Self-Defense: Taekwondo and basic martial arts training offered for physical fitness and confidence building.

Academic Clubs & Literary Societies

  • Himalayan Language Societies: Active reading and literary circles in Bhutia, Lepcha, Limbu, and Nepali to preserve regional scripts and dialects.
  • Science & Innovation Club: Annual exhibits featuring student projects in renewable energy, robotics, and regional ecological solutions.
  • Debate & Public Speaking: Inter-school debate programs and extempore competitions in English and regional languages.
  • School Periodical & Wall Magazine: Student-edited newsletters featuring original creative writing, poems, and regional artwork.

Youth Leadership & Community Service

  • Bharat Scouts & Guides: Training in leadership, first aid, knot-tying, camping, and disaster response drills.
  • Eco-Club (Green Enchey Initiative): Plastic-free campus drives, tree-plantation initiatives, and composting projects.
  • Community Outreach: Student-led social service visits to local health centers, community elder care, and clean-up campaigns across Gangtok town.

Infrastructure & Architectural Heritage

Campus & Architectural Heritage

  • The Sacred Site: Stands on land blessed by the 19th-century tantric master Lama Drupthob Karpo, who built a small hermitage here after reportedly flying from Maenam Hill in South Sikkim. This site later expanded into Enchey Monastery.
  • 100th Anniversary Celebrations (2009): Celebrated its centenary in October 2009, marking its journey from a 5-monk class in a monastery hut to a major educational institution.
  • Architecture & Setting: Situated on a ridge above Gangtok overlooking Mount Kanchenjunga, the campus incorporates traditional Sikkimese architectural motifs, including intricate woodwork, colorful window frames (Tashitakgye), and a traditional entrance gate (Ghyo).
  • Campus Facilities: Equipped with state-funded physics, chemistry, biology, and IT laboratories, residential hostels for rural students, and a library containing rare manuscripts and regional historical records.
